### You

You are a business analyst for Wildframe, a specialty coffee roaster that sells beans online and through a monthly subscription. Wildfram can run one four-week pop-up stand this spring, with a total rent budget of $5,000 for the four weeks. We need to determine which location offers the best opportunity for sales and profitability.
Data from comparable past events:
•	Riverside Farmers Market: 9,000 visitors per week; 2% of visitors buy; average sale $14 (mostly whole-bean bags); rent $900 per week.
•	Union Station concourse: 30,000 visitors per week; 0.5% of visitors buy; average sale $6 (mostly single cups); rent $2,000 per week.
•	Campus library atrium: 6,000 visitors per week; 3% of visitors buy; average sale $5 (single cups); rent $600 per week.
Work through this step by step before recommending:
1.	Calculate the expected weekly purchases and sales revenue for each location.
2.	Calculate the total projected revenue and rental costs over four weeks, identifying which locations fall within the $5,000 rent budget.
3.	Compare projected revenue after rent for each eligible location.
4.	Consider how each location's customer traffic, purchasing habits, and product preferences align with Wildframe's coffee business.
5.	Weigh the financial results against the opportunity to build brand awareness and attract future customers.
Then provide a brief explanation of your decision, including the biggest risk. End your response with a single line that starts with Recommendation: followed by the location's name.

# Wildframe Pop-Up Location Analysis

## Step 1: Weekly Purchases & Revenue

| Location | Weekly Visitors | Conversion | Buyers/Week | Avg Sale | Weekly Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Riverside Farmers Market | 9,000 | 2% | 180 | $14 | $2,520 |
| Union Station | 30,000 | 0.5% | 150 | $6 | $900 |
| Campus Library Atrium | 6,000 | 3% | 180 | $5 | $900 |

## Step 2: Four-Week Totals & Budget Check

| Location | 4-Wk Revenue | 4-Wk Rent | Within $5,000 Budget? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Riverside Farmers Market | $10,080 | $3,600 | ✅ Yes |
| Union Station | $3,600 | $8,000 | ❌ No — exceeds budget by $3,000 |
| Campus Library Atrium | $3,600 | $2,400 | ✅ Yes |

Union Station is eliminated outright — it blows the budget *and* delivers the lowest revenue of the three.

## Step 3: Revenue After Rent (Eligible Locations)

| Location | Revenue | Rent | Profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Riverside Farmers Market | $10,080 | $3,600 | **$6,480** |
| Campus Library Atrium | $3,600 | $2,400 | $1,200 |

Riverside outperforms Campus by over 5x in profit, while leaving $1,400 of the rent budget unspent.

## Step 4: Strategic Fit

- **Riverside** sells mostly **whole-bean bags** — the exact product our subscription is built around. Farmers market shoppers are also a strong demographic match for Wildframe's environmentally conscious, quality-focused customer base. This is prime territory for handing out subscription sign-up cards.
- **Campus** sells single cups to a largely student audience — good for sampling the brand, but lower revenue per customer and a demographic less likely to convert into recurring subscribers right now.
- **Union Station** offers huge raw traffic but a low-intent, single-cup commuter crowd — more "coffee on the way to work" than "coffee brand I'll fall in love with."

## Step 5: Brand Awareness Trade-off

Union Station's reach is tempting, but it's not financially accessible under this budget, and its low conversion rate suggests its audience isn't primed for a specialty roaster pitch anyway. Campus offers modest awareness-building among students (a future customer base) but weak near-term returns. Riverside delivers both the strongest financial outcome *and* a built-in audience of exactly the people who buy whole-bean bags and sign up for subscriptions — the best version of "brand awareness that actually converts."

## Decision & Risk

Riverside Farmers Market is the clear financial and strategic winner: nearly $6,500 in profit, the best product-market fit, and a customer base primed for subscription conversion. The main risk is weather and seasonal foot-traffic variability since it's an outdoor market — a few rained-out Saturdays could meaningfully dent both visitor counts and revenue. We should have a rain contingency (tent, earlier start time, or a backup indoor date) built into the plan.

Recommendation: Riverside Farmers Market

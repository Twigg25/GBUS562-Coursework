## What I Built
I completed a Prompt Lab using four different prompting techniques to solve marketing related business tasks for Wildframe, a specialty coffee company. The tasks included developing subscriber perks, creating coffee bean cards, responding to customer comments, and evaluating locations for a pop-up stand. I tested each prompt and reviewed the results to see how the different techniques affected the output.

## Why This Approach
Task 1 - Technique: zero-shot. I chose zero-shot prompting because I wanted the AI to brainstorm creative ideas without being influenced or limited by examples. The task was open-ended, so providing clear instructions about the brand, audience, and format was enough.  

Task 2 - Technique: one-shot. I chose one-shot prompting because I had a specific format the coffee cards needed to follow. Providing one completed example gave the AI a clear model for the layout, labels, and tone so the new cards would be consistent.  

Task 3 - Technique: few-shot. I chose few-shot prompting because I wanted the AI to learn Wildframe's brand voice from several examples. The different customer interactions demonstrated how the tone should adapt to complaints, questions, and compliments while still sounding like the same company.  

Task 4 - Technique: chain-of-thought. I chose chain-of-thought prompting because the task required analysis and decision-making rather than creative content. Breaking the process into steps helped the AI evaluate financial data, compare locations against the budget, and make a recommendation based on multiple factors.

## Reflection
Before this exercise, I didn't realize there were different prompting techniques or how much they could influence results. I learned that zero-shot works for brainstorming, one-shot for consistent formatting, and few-shot for establishing brand voice.

I chose Task 2 because I liked the creative coffee descriptions and how one example guided the results without limiting creativity.

Chain-of-thought made the biggest difference in the analytical task by breaking down the financial comparisons and supporting the recommendation.

I'm least confident in few-shot because the AI invented product details. I'd add clearer instructions to prevent that.

My takeaway is that effective prompting requires more intention than I realized.
